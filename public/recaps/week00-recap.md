## 1. Executive Summary & Core Philosophy

Week 0 establishes the guiding mental model and strategic diagnosis framework applied across the entire 12-week MBA/Executive course:
- **Think Strategically, Not in Isolation:** Your decision changes the incentives of others. Their strategic response changes your optimal decision.
- **What Game Theory Is:** A disciplined, mathematical, and conceptual framework to reason when business outcomes depend on interacting decisions. It enables leaders to anticipate strategic responses, detect stable outcomes and strategic traps, and structure incentives to reshape behavior.
- **What Game Theory Is NOT:** 
  - It is *not* a bag of manipulative tricks to "defeat" rivals.
  - It is *not* a magical guarantee that one best move always exists.
  - It is *not* abstract mathematics detached from executive P&L realities.

## 2. The 5-Step Strategic Diagnosis Framework

Throughout all 12 weeks, every business scenario is solved through this standardized 5-step diagnostic discipline:

```
1. Decision       --> Who must decide what?
2. Game           --> Who are the players, and what can each choose?
3. Structure      --> What are the timing (simultaneous vs. sequential), information, and incentives?
4. Analysis       --> What responses and outcomes does the model predict?
5. Recommendation --> What should the decision-maker do, and what assumption could reverse the conclusion?
```

> **Modelling Discipline Principle:**  
> *Select the model only after diagnosing the strategic problem.*

## 3. The Two Core Strategic Representations

| Tool | Formal Representation | When to Use | Introduction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Payoff Matrix** | Normal / Strategic Form table of interacting choices | Players choose without observing one another's current choices (simultaneous moves) | Formalized in **Week 1** |
| **Game Tree** | Extensive Form decision nodes, branches, and terminal payoff vectors | Decisions occur in order, and later players observe earlier actions (sequential moves) | Formalized in **Week 2** |

*Rule:* The representation must match the timing and information structure of the actual business decision.

## 4. Key Business Inquiries Across the Curriculum

1. **Price Wars:** Why do rational firms cut prices even when a price war destroys joint industry profits? (Weeks 1 & 3)
2. **Strategic Preemption & Commitment:** When does a billion-dollar capital expenditure deter rival entry, and when does it backfire? (Weeks 2 & 5)
3. **Auctions & Allocations:** How should a company bid for spectrum, land, or search ads without falling prey to the Winner's Curse? (Weeks 7 & 8)
4. **Bargaining Power:** What determines value capture in M&A acquisitions, supplier negotiations, or VC term sheets? (Week 9)
5. **Platform Competition:** How do digital ecosystems manage cross-side network effects, multihoming, and coopetition? (Weeks 11 & 12)

## 6. How to Study & Master Game Theory

1. Answer the visible strategic dilemma before reading the solution.
2. Reconstruct the payoff matrix, game tree, or strategic calculation without looking.
3. Change one underlying assumption and test whether the predicted equilibrium reverses.
4. Conclude with a clear one-sentence executive recommendation accompanied by its critical boundary condition.
